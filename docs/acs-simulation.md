# ACS scenario: gameplay assumptions and clinical review

Status: **pending clinical review; fictional simulation, not clinical guidance**.

The scenario uses a fixed abnormal ECG and troponin as illustrative case data. Not all patients with ACS have ST elevation or an initially raised troponin; a normal initial result does not exclude ACS. Aspirin must not be assumed appropriate in every patient. Contraindications and individualized antiplatelet/reperfusion decisions are not fully modelled.

The untreated 120-second and post-aspirin 600-second VF transitions are **arbitrary gameplay timers**, not clinical estimates or evidence that aspirin postpones VF by a specific amount. Completing the aspirin order delays this scripted event but does not prevent it. This simplified rule does not model definitive reperfusion, alternative antiplatelets, or individual risk and requires clinical review before educational use.

Background references: 2025 ACC/AHA acute coronary syndromes guideline, https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001309 ; ANZCOR initial medical therapy, https://www.anzcor.org/home/acute-coronary-syndromes/guideline-14-2-acute-coronary-syndromes-initial-medical-therapy . The gameplay timer is not sourced from either guideline.

In the general-ward version, the one-shot 5% VF chance and the 30- or 60-minute assessment points are also **arbitrary placeholders**, not patient-level clinical predictions. The game has no subsequent recurrent-risk model after the one-shot evaluation; ongoing chest pain remains. The original faster scenario remains available separately for testing. These values and the simplified antiplatelet-dependent timing need clinical review.
